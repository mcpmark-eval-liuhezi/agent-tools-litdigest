# Agentic Tool Use in Large Language Models

- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00835v2
- **Title:** Agentic Tool Use in Large Language Models
- **Authors:** Jinchao Hu, Meizhi Zhong, Kehai Chen, Xuefeng Bai, Min Zhang
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00835

## Summary

This survey argues that research on tool use in LLM agents is fragmented across tasks, tool types, and training regimes, and that prior reviews give only static snapshots of it. It reorganizes the field into three evolutionary paradigms — prompting as plug-and-play, supervised tool learning, and reward-driven tool policy learning — with the boundaries between them drawn by what optimization signal is used: in-context control, labeled or synthetic supervision, or reward feedback. For each paradigm it catalogs representative methods (ReAct/Reflexion through ToolLLM/Toolformer to RL-based tool policies), their strengths and failure modes, then treats evaluation as a cross-cutting dimension that has matured from function-call correctness to end-to-end interactive success, safety, and robustness, and closes on open problems such as long-horizon credit assignment, scalable generalization to unseen tools, and alignment-aware deployment.
